🧪 Zero Conditional

Used for real facts, scientific truths, or general routines. If A happens, B always happens.

Ex: If you heat ice, it melts. 🧊

⚡ First Conditional

Used for future possibilities or probable consequences in a specific situation.

Ex: If I finish tasks, I will win. 🏆

🧪 Zero Conditional

The Zero Conditional is used to express universal truths, scientific facts, habits, or general rules. 🌍

Grammar Structure 📐
If / When + Present Simple , Present Simple

📌 Practical Spaceship Examples:

"If you press the emergency button, a meeting starts." 🚨

(General rule: pressing the button always triggers an emergency meeting.)

"If water reaches 100 degrees Celsius, it boils." ♨️

(Scientific fact: water always boils at 100°C.)

"If crewmates do not complete tasks, the team loses." ❌

(Game mechanic: uncompleted tasks always result in defeat.)

⚡ First Conditional

The First Conditional is used to talk about real or very probable situations in the future and their expected outcomes. 🔮

Grammar Structure 📐
If + Present Simple , WILL + Verb (base form)

📌 Practical Spaceship Examples:

"If I find the impostor, I will report it immediately." 🔍

(Probable future event and its specific consequence.)

"If you go to Electrical alone, the impostor will eliminate you." ⚡

(Future prediction based on a condition.)

"If we study grammar today, we will pass the spaceship exam." 🎓

(Future result dependent on today's action.)
